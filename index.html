Labour & Employment Laws

India’s new Labour Codes

India has consolidated 29 central labour laws into four Labour Codes, which came into force on 21 November 2025. Together, they create a more unified framework for wages, industrial relations, workplace safety and social security, while changing important compliance responsibilities for employers and establishments.

Code 01

Code on Wages, 2019

Covers minimum wages, payment of wages and bonuses, wage-related definitions and equal remuneration principles.

Code 02

Industrial Relations Code, 2020

Deals with industrial disputes, trade unions, standing orders, retrenchment, layoffs, closure and other employment-relations matters.

Code 03

Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020

Provides the framework for workplace safety, health, welfare, working conditions, registration and related employer obligations.

Code 04

Code on Social Security, 2020

Brings together social-security provisions including EPF, ESI, gratuity, maternity benefits and a framework extending to gig and platform workers.

What this means for employers

The new framework requires businesses to review how they manage workforce records, wage structures, registrations, social-security obligations and workplace compliance. The precise requirement can vary according to the establishment, workforce, applicable rules and state-level provisions.

Electronic compliance
Registration, records and filings are increasingly standardised and digitised.
Revised wage concepts
Definitions can affect payroll, benefits, gratuity and social-security calculations.
Higher compliance risk
Businesses should review policies and procedures to reduce regulatory and legal exposure.
Legal Awareness

Rights every Indian should know.

This section is designed as a short public-awareness resource. It provides general information only and should not be treated as case-specific legal advice.

Right to EqualityConstitutional protection against unlawful discrimination and unequal treatment by the State, subject to recognised constitutional exceptions.
Right to Life & Personal LibertyProtection of life and personal liberty except according to procedure established by law, interpreted through extensive constitutional jurisprudence.
Access to Legal RemediesCourts and legal-services institutions provide avenues for enforcement of legal and constitutional rights, depending on the nature of the matter.
Consumer ProtectionConsumers have statutory remedies relating to defective goods, deficient services, unfair trade practices and certain misleading representations.
Workplace RightsEmployees and workers may have rights relating to wages, safety, social security, maternity benefits, employment conditions and dispute resolution.
Criminal Law ProtectionsIndia’s current criminal-law framework includes the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam.
